TITLE: Electric Vehicles and Tailpipe Emissions
SOURCE TYPE: Environmental research briefing
TOPIC: Impact of Electric Vehicles on the Environment

1. TAILPIPE EMISSIONS

Battery electric vehicles (BEVs) do not have an internal combustion engine and do
not burn fuel while driving. As a direct result, battery electric vehicles produce
zero tailpipe emissions. They emit no carbon dioxide, no nitrogen oxides, and no
particulate matter from an exhaust pipe during operation.

It is important to state this precisely. "Zero tailpipe emissions" refers only to
the emissions released at the vehicle itself while it is being driven. It does not
mean the vehicle is responsible for zero emissions overall.

2. URBAN AIR QUALITY

Because they do not emit exhaust gases at street level, electric vehicles can reduce
urban air pollution. Cities with a high share of electric vehicles have measured
lower concentrations of nitrogen dioxide and fine particulate matter along busy
roads. This is particularly relevant for dense city centres where traffic is the
dominant source of local air pollution.

Reduced local pollution is associated with public health benefits, including fewer
respiratory illnesses in populations living close to major roads.

3. NOT ALL EMISSIONS ARE ELIMINATED

Electric vehicles still generate some non-exhaust particulate matter. Brake wear,
tyre wear, and road surface abrasion release particles regardless of the type of
powertrain. Regenerative braking in electric vehicles can reduce brake wear, but
tyre wear may be comparable to or higher than conventional vehicles because electric
vehicles are often heavier due to their battery packs.

4. DEPENDENCE ON ELECTRICITY GENERATION

The overall environmental impact of an electric vehicle depends partly on how the
electricity used to charge it is generated. When an electric vehicle is charged from
a grid dominated by coal-fired power stations, the emissions are shifted from the
vehicle to the power plant rather than eliminated. When the grid uses a large share
of renewable or nuclear generation, the associated emissions are considerably lower.

Therefore, an electric vehicle should be described as having zero tailpipe emissions,
not as having zero emissions or zero environmental impact.
